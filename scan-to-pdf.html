Scan Documents to PDF with Your Phone Camera — Free Online Scanner

Turn your phone or computer into a document scanner. Point your camera at a page, capture a photo, and this tool corrects the perspective, enhances the image, and saves everything as a clean multi-page PDF. No app install, no account, no file uploads — the entire process runs in your browser.

Camera Scanning vs. Image Upload

There are two ways to get pages into the scanner:

  • Camera scanning — on a phone or tablet with a camera, the scanner opens a live camera view. You frame the document, tap the capture button, and the page is added to your document. This is the fastest way to scan physical paper.
  • Image upload — on a desktop, or when you already have photos of documents, you can drop or select image files (JPG, PNG, WEBP, HEIC). Each image becomes a page in the final PDF.

You can mix both: scan a few pages with the camera, then add existing photos from your gallery or file system. All pages appear together in the page grid and are combined into one PDF.

Auto-Detect and Manual Crop

After a capture, the scanner tries to find the edges of the document automatically. When it succeeds, four blue corner handles snap to the corners of the paper. You can accept the result as-is, or drag any handle to fine-tune it.

If auto-detection does not find the document — for example, on a low-contrast page or a busy background — the corners default to the frame edges. Tap Auto Detect to try again, or drag the handles manually. A magnifier appears near your finger while dragging so you can line up each corner precisely. Tap Reset to return the corners to the frame edges.

Perspective Correction

When you photograph a page at an angle, the rectangle of the paper becomes a trapezoid in the image. Perspective correction (also called a perspective transform or homography) maps those four corners back to a true rectangle, so the output page looks like a flat scan instead of a tilted photo.

This is what the Continue button does after you set the corners. The four corner points are used to compute a transform matrix, and the image is warped so the document fills the output rectangle. Without this step, a phone photo of a page would have visible keystone distortion.

Filters and Enhancements

Raw phone photos of paper often have uneven lighting, colour casts, and shadows. The Enhance screen gives you a set of filters and sliders to clean up the page before export:

  • Original — no processing. Use this when the photo is already clean.
  • Document — grayscale conversion, illumination normalization to remove shadows, and a whiteness boost to push the paper toward pure white. This is the default and works well for most printed pages.
  • B&W — grayscale, illumination normalization, and adaptive thresholding. Produces a high-contrast black-and-white image suitable for text-only documents.
  • Grayscale — removes colour but keeps tone. Good for photos or pages with images.
  • Photo — minimal processing, keeps colour and detail. Use for pages that are themselves photographs or artwork.

The four sliders let you fine-tune any filter:

  • Brightness — shifts the overall lightness of the page.
  • Contrast — increases or decreases the difference between light and dark areas.
  • Sharpness — applies an unsharp mask to make text edges crisper.
  • Whiteness — pushes near-white pixels to pure white, cleaning up grey paper.

Page Order, Editing, and Rotation

The Pages screen shows every captured or imported page as a thumbnail. From here you can:

  • Reorder — drag a page card to a new position. The order of the cards is the order of pages in the exported PDF.
  • Edit — tap a page to reopen the Enhance screen for that page. You can change the filter, adjust the sliders, or re-crop.
  • Delete — tap the ✕ on any page to remove it.
  • Add more — tap the “+ Add Page” card to return to the camera or the upload screen and add another page.

If a page was captured sideways, you can rotate it from the Enhance screen before exporting. Rotation is applied to the final page in the PDF.

Compression and PDF Quality

Before exporting, choose a quality preset. Each preset caps the longest edge of the page image and sets the JPEG compression level used when embedding the image into the PDF:

  • Small — longest edge 1200px, JPEG quality 0.65. Best for email attachments and web uploads where file size matters.
  • Recommended — longest edge 2000px, JPEG quality 0.85. A good balance for most everyday documents.
  • High — longest edge 3000px, JPEG quality 0.95. Best when you need to print the scan or preserve fine detail.

You can also choose the PDF page size. Fit to image makes each PDF page exactly the size of the scanned image. A4, Letter, and Legal place the image on a standard page size, centred, with the image scaled to fit while preserving its aspect ratio.

Privacy — Scanning Without Uploads

Every step — camera capture, perspective correction, filtering, and PDF generation — runs locally in your browser using JavaScript and the open-source pdf-lib library. Your images are never uploaded to a server. Once the page has loaded, you can even disconnect from the internet and continue scanning.

The camera stream stays on your device. The only time the tool needs a network connection is when it first loads its libraries from a CDN.

Practical Examples

Scanning a contract to send by email: Capture each page, let auto-detect find the corners, apply the Document filter, reorder if needed, set quality to Small, and export as PDF.

Digitising handwritten notes: Use the B&W filter with a slightly higher whiteness value to make the text stand out. Export at Recommended quality.

Turning a photo of a receipt into a PDF: Load the photo from your gallery, crop to the receipt edges, apply Photo or Document filter, and export.

Combining phone photos and a scanned page: Scan the paper pages with the camera, then use “+ Add Page” to import a photo you already have. Both end up in the same PDF.

Scan to PDF Questions

Do I need to install an app to scan documents to PDF?

No. This tool runs entirely in your web browser. On a phone, tap the camera button on the Scan screen to capture a page. On a desktop, drop or select image files. Either way, the PDF is generated in your browser and downloaded directly to your device.

How does auto-detect find the edges of my document?

The scanner runs an edge-detection algorithm (Canny) on the live camera frame, finds the largest four-corner contour, and orders the corners as top-left, top-right, bottom-right, and bottom-left. If the detected shape looks like a rectangular document covering most of the frame, the corners are used as the starting crop. If nothing convincing is found, the crop defaults to the frame edges and you can drag the handles yourself.

What is perspective correction and why do I need it?

When you photograph a page at an angle, the rectangle of the paper appears as a trapezoid in the photo. Perspective correction maps the four corners of that trapezoid back to a true rectangle, so the output looks like a flat scan. Without it, a tilted photo would produce a PDF page with visible keystone distortion.

Can I rotate a page after scanning it?

Yes. Open the page in the Enhance screen and use the rotation controls. The rotated page is saved back to the page list and used in the final PDF.

How do I change the order of pages in the PDF?

On the Pages screen, drag any page card to a new position. The order of the cards from left-to-right, top-to-bottom becomes the page order in the exported PDF.

Which filter should I use for a printed document?

The Document filter is the best starting point for most printed pages. It converts to grayscale, removes uneven lighting, and brightens the paper. If the text is faint or the scan is noisy, try the B&W filter with adaptive thresholding.

What does the Whiteness slider do?

Whiteness pushes pixels that are already close to white all the way to pure white. This cleans up grey backgrounds, faint paper texture, and scanner noise, making text stand out. Higher values make the background cleaner but can erase light-coloured content if pushed too far.

Can I scan directly to a PDF on my phone?

Yes. Open this page on your phone’s browser, allow camera access, and capture each page. The scanner builds the PDF in your browser and downloads it to your phone. No app install and no account are required.

How large will the PDF be?

File size depends on the quality preset and the number of pages. Small quality typically produces 100–300 KB per page; Recommended is usually 300–800 KB per page; High can be 1–2 MB per page for dense text or photos. For a typical multi-page document, Recommended gives a good balance.

Does the tool keep my images after I close the tab?

No. Everything is held in your browser’s memory while the page is open. Closing or refreshing the tab discards the captured pages, and nothing is stored on any server. If you want to keep a scan, export the PDF before closing the tab.

Can I scan a document that has both text and photos?

Yes. Use the Document filter for text-heavy pages and the Photo filter for pages that are mostly images. You can set the filter per page in the Enhance screen, so a mixed document can have different treatments on different pages.

What image formats can I import?

Any image format the browser can decode: JPG, JPEG, PNG, WEBP, GIF, BMP, and HEIC/HEIF on browsers that support it (Safari natively, some Android browsers via extensions). The imported image is added to the page list and can be cropped, filtered, and reordered just like a camera capture.
