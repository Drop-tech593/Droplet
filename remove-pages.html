Removing pages from a PDF: the complete guide

Removing pages from a PDF is one of the most common document tasks in professional life. A contract arrives with an extra signature page. A report has blank pages from a double-sided print. A scanned book has a table of contents you do not need. A tender submission needs to exclude the confidential pricing annex. In every case, the right answer is the same: open the PDF, mark the pages you want gone, save the rest.

The operation itself is straightforward — a PDF is a container, and pages are discrete objects inside it. Removing a page means writing a new file that references every page except the one you want to leave out. What makes it non-trivial is that a PDF page is not a single object. It is a dictionary that points at a content stream, a resource dictionary, a set of fonts, a set of images, and often a set of annotations. A correct removal tool has to copy all of these correctly into the new file so that the pages you keep look exactly as they did before.

This tool does that. It reads the original PDF, walks the page tree, and writes a new PDF that contains only the pages you kept. Text stays selectable, links keep working, vector graphics stay sharp at any zoom, and embedded fonts render identically. It runs entirely in your browser — nothing is uploaded.

When you should remove pages

  • Strip cover sheets. Many scanned documents begin with a routing or cover page you do not need in the final version.
  • Delete blank pages. Double-sided scans frequently produce blank pages between chapters. This tool highlights likely-blank pages so you can remove them all quickly.
  • Remove confidential sections. When part of a document contains pricing, personal data, or commercial terms that should not be shared, remove those pages before distributing.
  • Clean up after merging. If you merged several PDFs and ended up with duplicate cover pages, remove the extras.
  • Trim a report. Long reports often carry appendices or reference sections that the recipient does not need.
  • Fix a print order. If a print run produced a document with the pages in the wrong order, remove the misplaced pages and re-add them.

How to use this tool

Step 1. Upload your PDF. Drag it onto the drop zone or click Select PDF File.

Step 2. Mark pages for removal. Every page starts green (kept). Click a thumbnail to change it to red (removed). Click again to bring it back to green. Use Shift-click to mark a range. Or type a range in the text box — for example 3-7, 12 — and press Enter.

Step 3. Verify the preview. The strip underneath the grid shows every page in order, with the ones marked for removal faded out. Green borders mean kept; red borders mean removed. Every kept page shows a badge with its position in the output.

Step 4. Reorder kept pages if you want. Drag a kept thumbnail to a new position. The numbers update immediately.

Step 5. Export. Press Remove Pages. The cleaned PDF downloads as dropletweb-<original-name>-kept.pdf. If you also want the kept pages as individual files, press Remove and export kept pages as ZIP instead.

Three selection methods

Click is the most direct. Click any thumbnail to toggle it between kept and removed. Useful for removing one or two specific pages.

Shift-click extends the selection. Click one page to mark it removed, then hold Shift and click another page further down. Every page between the two is marked removed. Useful for removing a contiguous block.

Typed ranges are the fastest way to specify exactly which pages to remove. The parser accepts a comma-separated list of numbers and ranges: 1, 3-5, 8, 12-14. Reversed ranges like 10-3 are treated as 3 through 10. Duplicates are ignored. Whitespace is optional.

Why client-side matters

Most online PDF tools upload your file to a server, process it there, and send the result back. That means a machine you do not control has a copy of your document — even if only for a few seconds. For contracts, financial statements, medical records, or anything under a confidentiality obligation, that is a real risk. This tool never does that. The PDF stays in your browser's memory, the page removal happens on your CPU, and the output file is generated locally. You can verify it yourself: open your browser's developer tools, go to the Network tab, and watch as you remove pages. Nothing gets sent anywhere.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my PDF safe?

Yes. All processing happens locally in your browser. Your PDF is never uploaded to any server and is removed from memory when you close the page.

Is this tool free?

Yes, the tool is completely free and does not require a sign-up or account.

How do I remove a single page?

Every page starts kept. Click the thumbnail of the page you want to remove. It becomes tinted red. Then press Remove Pages to download the cleaned PDF.

How do I remove a range of pages?

Click the first page of the range, then hold Shift and click the last page. Every page between them is marked for removal. Or type a range like 3-7, 12 in the text box and press Enter.

Can I undo the removal after export?

No, exporting produces a new file. The original PDF on your device is untouched, so you can always start again from the original.

Can I remove all pages except one?

Yes. Click Remove all to mark every page for removal, then click the one page you want to keep. It becomes green again, and all others stay red.

Can I remove blank pages automatically?

Droplet highlights likely-blank pages with a small orange badge so you can identify them quickly. There is no automatic removal yet — you still click each one to mark it — but the badge makes it fast.

Can I reorder the pages that remain?

Yes. Drag any kept thumbnail to a new position. The order you see is the order they will appear in the output.

What is the maximum file size?

The tool accepts PDFs up to 100 MB. Because everything runs locally, the practical limit is your device's available memory.

Does it work on encrypted PDFs?

Password-protected PDFs must be unlocked before they can be edited. Remove the password first, then use this tool.

Will the text remain selectable in the cleaned PDF?

Yes. Pages are copied as native PDF objects, so text, links, vector graphics, and embedded fonts are preserved exactly.

Does it work offline?

Once the page has loaded, all parsing and extraction run locally. You can remove pages without an internet connection.
