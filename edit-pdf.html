Working…

Edit PDF files online, free and private

Droplet's PDF Editor runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded. You open a PDF, the tool reads its content locally, and you edit, sign, highlight, and export — all on your own device. No account. No server. No waiting for uploads.

What you can do

  • Edit existing text. Every piece of text in the PDF becomes an editable object. Click it, change it, restyle it.
  • Add new text. Pick a font, size, colour, alignment, and line height. Type directly on the page.
  • Insert images and signatures. Upload a PNG, JPG, or SVG, place it anywhere, resize by dragging.
  • Highlight and whiteout. Mark up sections of a document or hide content behind a white rectangle.
  • Draw and add shapes. Freehand drawing, rectangles, ellipses, lines, and arrows.
  • Insert form fields. Text boxes, checkboxes, radio buttons, dropdowns, dates, and signature fields.
  • OCR scanned pages. Detect when a page is scanned and place an editable text overlay using OCR.
  • Manage pages. Rotate, duplicate, delete, or add blank pages.
  • Undo and redo. Every action is recorded in a full history stack.

Three export formats

The same document can leave the editor in three different shapes:

  • PDF — the same PDF with your edits baked in. Text stays as text, images stay as images, shapes stay as vector.
  • DOCX — a Word document with each page as a section. Text becomes native Word runs with the right fonts and sizes. Images and shapes are embedded as inline drawings.
  • XLSX — an Excel workbook with one sheet per page. Each text object becomes a row with columns for position, size, font, colour, and content. Useful for auditing, indexing, or feeding content into another system.

Why client-side matters

When you upload a PDF to a server-based editor, that server sees your document. Contracts, medical records, financial statements, and identity documents pass through a machine you do not control. Droplet's editor never does that. The PDF stays in your browser's memory, the edits happen on your CPU, and the exported file is generated locally. You can verify this by opening your browser's Network tab while editing — nothing leaves the page.

Frequently asked questions

Is this PDF editor free?

Yes. The editor is completely free and does not require a sign-up or account.

Is my PDF safe?

Yes. All processing happens locally in your browser. Your PDF is never uploaded to any server and is removed from memory when you close the page.

Can I edit the existing text in a PDF?

Yes. When you open a PDF, Droplet converts the embedded text into editable objects. Click any text to edit it. The original text underneath is masked with a colour sampled from its background so your edit is what shows.

What export formats are supported?

PDF (native), DOCX (Word document with text, images, and shapes), and XLSX (Excel workbook with one sheet per page, each object as a row).

Can I add a signature to a PDF?

Yes. Select the Signature tool and click where you want the signature. You can upload an image of your signature or draw one on the page.

Does it work on scanned PDFs?

Yes. Scanned pages are detected automatically. Use the OCR button to place an editable text overlay on top of the scan.

Does it work offline?

Once the page has loaded, all parsing, editing, and export run locally. You can edit PDFs without an internet connection.

Is there a maximum file size?

The tool accepts PDFs up to 50 MB. Because everything runs locally, the practical limit is your device's available memory.

Can I undo my edits?

Yes. Every action is recorded in the undo history. Use Ctrl+Z to undo and Ctrl+Y to redo.

Does the DOCX export keep my formatting?

The DOCX export preserves text, fonts, sizes, and colours as native Word formatting. Images, shapes, and highlights are exported as inline drawings.

What does the XLSX export contain?

The XLSX export creates one sheet per page. Each text object becomes a row with its position, size, font, colour, and content. Images, shapes, and annotations are listed with their type and position.

Can I add form fields?

Yes. Select the Forms tool and click on the page to insert a form field placeholder. Types include text, checkbox, radio, dropdown, date, and signature.
