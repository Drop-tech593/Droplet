How Word to PDF Conversion Works

This tool reads your .docx file in the browser using the open-source Mammoth library, converts the document structure into HTML, and then renders that HTML into a PDF using html2pdf. The result is a PDF that looks like the Word document and preserves most of its formatting.

The process runs in three stages: parse the DOCX, build the HTML, then rasterise the HTML page by page into the final PDF. Everything happens locally on your device.

DOCX Rendering and Fonts

Word documents use the fonts that were set by the document's author. When the file is converted in your browser, those fonts must be available on your machine for the PDF to render correctly. If you created the DOCX on a computer with a custom font that is not installed on the machine running the conversion, the browser substitutes a similar font.

The converter applies a clean serif fallback — Times New Roman for body text, Georgia for the preview — so the PDF remains readable even when the original font is missing. Headings keep their size hierarchy: level 1 headings render at 18 pt, level 2 at 15 pt, and level 3 at 12 pt.

Font styling that survives: bold, italic, underline, size, and colour for headings. Font styling that may not survive perfectly: exact kerning, ligatures, and fonts that are embedded in the DOCX but not installed on your system.

Tables

Tables in the DOCX are parsed by Mammoth and rebuilt as HTML tables. The converter then applies consistent styling: 1-pixel borders, padding, and a header background where the DOCX specified one.

Simple tables convert cleanly. Tables with:

  • Fixed column widths set in the DOCX — the widths are approximated but may not match exactly.
  • Merged cells — the merge is preserved as a colspan or rowspan in HTML.
  • Nested tables — usually render, though complex nesting can occasionally break.
  • Shading, borders, or conditional formatting — only basic borders and background colours are preserved.

Tables are configured to avoid splitting across pages when possible, so a table that fits on one page usually stays on one page in the PDF.

Images

Images embedded in the DOCX are extracted by Mammoth and re-embedded into the HTML as base64 data URLs, so they do not depend on any external file. They then render into the PDF at their native resolution.

Images are constrained to a maximum width of 100% of the content area, so they never overflow the page. Very large images are scaled down proportionally. Small images stay at their original size, centred on the page.

What does not work: linked images (where the DOCX references an external file path), and images inside headers or footers, which are not part of the body content and are usually stripped by the DOCX parser.

Page Layout — What Changes

Word is a reflowable format: text flows and wraps based on the page size, margins, and available fonts. The converter approximates Word's layout but will not reproduce it exactly.

Element What Happens
Page size You choose A4, Letter, or Legal. The DOCX's original page size is not read.
Orientation Portrait or landscape, your choice. The DOCX's orientation is not read.
Margins 20 mm on all sides. The DOCX's custom margins are not preserved.
Line breaks Soft line breaks (from word wrap) are recalculated. Hard line breaks (from Enter) are preserved.
Page breaks Explicit page breaks in the DOCX are preserved. Automatic page breaks are recalculated.
Columns Multi-column layouts are not supported. Content reflows into a single column.
Headers and footers Not preserved. The PDF does not include header or footer content from the DOCX.
Footnotes and endnotes May appear inline at the end of the document rather than as linked notes.
Styles and themes Word style names are not preserved. Direct formatting (bold, italic, size) is.

What Is Not Supported

  • .doc (legacy Word format) — only .docx is supported.
  • Password-protected DOCX — remove the password first.
  • Track changes and comments — the accepted version is converted; tracked changes and comment bubbles are not.
  • Embedded fonts — if the DOCX embeds a font that is not installed on your machine, the browser substitutes it.
  • Macros and VBA — DOCM files with macros are not executed and may not parse.
  • Charts and SmartArt — these are stored as separate objects in the DOCX and are not rendered into the PDF.

Quality and File Size

The quality setting controls how much JPEG compression is applied to the rendered pages. Standard (0.6) produces the smallest files. High (0.85) is the default and works well for most documents. Maximum (0.95) is best for printing or when the document contains fine detail.

Because the PDF is built from rasterised page images, file size depends mostly on page count and quality. A 10-page document at Maximum quality can be several megabytes.

Privacy — Client-Side Conversion

DOCX parsing uses the open-source Mammoth library and PDF generation uses html2pdf. Both run entirely in your browser. Your file is read into memory and never transmitted. The only external resources loaded are the libraries themselves from a CDN.

Practical Examples

Sharing a report as a PDF: Upload the DOCX, choose A4, Portrait, and High quality. The result is a professional-looking PDF that preserves headings, paragraphs, and tables.

Submitting a CV or resume: Upload the DOCX, choose A4, and High quality. Fonts render cleanly because they are usually standard fonts that are installed everywhere.

Archiving a document with images: Upload the DOCX, choose Letter or A4, and Maximum quality. Images are embedded at full resolution.

Creating a printable handout: Upload the DOCX, choose A4, Portrait, Wide margins via the page size defaults, and Maximum quality.

Word to PDF Questions

Is my data safe?

Yes. All conversion happens locally in your browser. Your Word document is never uploaded to any server. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and the tool will continue to work.

Will the PDF look exactly like the Word document?

No. Word is reflowable and the PDF is built from a rasterised approximation of the document. Headings, paragraphs, tables, and images keep their structure, but exact line breaks, page breaks, and font metrics will differ. Fonts that are not installed on your machine are substituted.

What file types are supported?

Only .docx (Word 2007 and later). The legacy .doc format is not supported. If you have a .doc file, save it as .docx in Word first.

Are tables preserved?

Yes, simple and moderately complex tables are preserved. Cell borders, padding, and header rows render cleanly. Very complex tables — with merged cells spanning many rows, nested tables, or conditional formatting — may have minor visual differences.

Are images preserved?

Yes. Embedded images are extracted and re-inserted at their native resolution. Images are scaled to fit the content width, so they never overflow the page. Linked images (where the DOCX references an external file path) are not preserved.

Will my custom fonts be preserved?

Only if the font is installed on the machine running the conversion. If the DOCX uses a custom font that is not installed on your system, the browser substitutes a similar one. The PDF remains readable, but the exact letterforms may differ.

Are headers and footers preserved?

No. Headers, footers, and their content (page numbers, running titles, logos) are not part of the body content and are not converted. The PDF does not include them.

Is my document uploaded to a server?

No. All parsing and PDF generation happen locally in your browser using Mammoth and html2pdf. Your file never leaves your device.

Can I convert a password-protected DOCX?

No. Remove the password first using Word or a dedicated tool, then upload the file here.

What about charts and SmartArt?

Charts and SmartArt are stored as separate objects in the DOCX and are not rendered into the PDF. If your document relies on them, export it to PDF directly from Word instead.

Can I choose the page size?

Yes. A4, Letter, and Legal are available, in portrait or landscape orientation. The DOCX's original page size and orientation are not read; the PDF uses the size you choose.

How can I improve the output quality?

Use the Maximum quality setting, ensure that any custom fonts used in the document are installed on your machine, and keep the original page size (A4 or Letter) matching what the document was designed for.
