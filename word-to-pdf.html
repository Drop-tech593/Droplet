How Word to PDF Conversion Works

This tool reads your .docx file in the browser using the open-source Mammoth library, converts the document structure into HTML, then paginates the content into real A4/Letter/Legal pages before rendering them into a PDF.

Unlike simple HTML-to-PDF converters, this tool measures every block — headings, paragraphs, images, and table rows — and decides where each page boundary should fall. That produces clean pagination without mid-sentence cuts or giant blank areas.

DOCX Rendering and Fonts

Word documents use the fonts that were set by the author. When the file is converted in your browser, those fonts must be available on your machine for the PDF to render correctly. If you created the DOCX with a custom font that is not installed locally, the browser substitutes a similar font.

The converter applies a clean serif fallback — Times New Roman for body text — so the PDF remains readable even when the original font is missing. Headings keep their size hierarchy: level 1 renders at 18 pt, level 2 at 15 pt, and level 3 at 12 pt.

Tables

Tables are parsed by Mammoth and rebuilt as HTML tables. During pagination, a table that doesn't fully fit in the remaining space on a page continues onto the next page — split cleanly between rows. Individual rows are never chopped in half.

Simple tables convert cleanly. Tables with fixed column widths, merged cells, or nested tables usually render, but very complex layouts may have minor visual differences from the original DOCX.

Images

Images embedded in the DOCX are extracted and re-embedded into the HTML as base64 data URLs. They render into the PDF at their native resolution, scaled to fit the content area so they never overflow the page.

What Is Not Supported

  • .doc (legacy Word format) — only .docx is supported.
  • Password-protected DOCX — remove the password first.
  • Track changes and comments — the accepted version is converted.
  • Macros and VBA — DOCM files may not parse.
  • Charts and SmartArt — stored as separate objects and not rendered.

Quality and File Size

The quality setting controls JPEG compression on each rendered page. Standard (0.6) produces the smallest files. High (0.85) is the default and works well for most documents. Maximum (0.95) is best for printing or when the document contains fine detail.

Privacy — Client-Side Conversion

DOCX parsing uses Mammoth and PDF generation uses html2pdf with a custom pagination engine. Both run entirely in your browser. Your file is read into memory and never transmitted.

Word to PDF Questions

Is my data safe?

Yes. All conversion happens locally in your browser. Your Word document is never uploaded to any server. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and the tool will continue to work.

Will the PDF look exactly like the Word document?

No. Word is reflowable and the PDF is a paginated approximation. Headings, paragraphs, tables, and images keep their structure, but exact line breaks, page breaks, and font metrics will differ.

What file types are supported?

Only .docx (Word 2007 and later).

Are tables preserved?

Yes. Tables are split between rows across pages — no row is chopped in half, and no table is forced to move as one giant block. Header rows may or may not repeat depending on the source DOCX.

Are images preserved?

Yes. Embedded images are extracted and re-inserted at their native resolution, scaled to fit the content area.

Will my custom fonts be preserved?

Only if the font is installed on the machine running the conversion. Otherwise the browser substitutes a similar one.

Are headers and footers preserved?

No. Headers, footers, and their content are not converted.

Is my document uploaded to a server?

No. Everything runs locally in your browser.

Can I convert a password-protected DOCX?

No. Remove the password first.

What about charts and SmartArt?

Charts and SmartArt are not rendered into the PDF. Export the document directly from Word if you need those preserved.

Can I choose the page size?

Yes. A4, Letter, and Legal are available, in portrait or landscape orientation.

How can I improve the output quality?

Use the Maximum quality setting, install any custom fonts locally, and match the page size to what the document was designed for.
