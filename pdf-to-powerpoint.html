How Each PDF Page Becomes a Slide

The conversion is straightforward. For every page in the PDF, the tool renders that page as a high-resolution image at the quality you selected, then places that image on its own PowerPoint slide. A 12-page PDF produces a 12-slide presentation, in the same order as the original pages.

The image is scaled to fit the slide while preserving its aspect ratio. If the PDF page is wider than the slide, it is centred vertically. If the PDF page is taller than the slide, it is centred horizontally. A thin white margin may appear on the sides or top and bottom where the aspect ratios do not match.

Each slide has a white background. The PDF page image sits on top of that background at the position calculated to fit. The result is a presentation that looks like the original PDF, slide by slide.

What Is Editable and What Is Not

This is the most important thing to understand about this tool. The conversion is raster-based, not structural. The PDF page is turned into a picture, and that picture is placed on the slide.

  • Not editable: the text inside the page image. You cannot click into a heading and change a word, because the "heading" is just pixels. The same goes for images, tables, and diagrams that were part of the PDF page.
  • Editable: anything you add on top of the slide in PowerPoint. You can insert text boxes, arrows, shapes, highlights, hyperlinks, and speaker notes. You can also change the slide order, delete slides, duplicate them, or apply a theme.
  • Editable: the slide size and layout. You choose between 16:9 widescreen and 4:3 standard before conversion. Both are real PowerPoint slide dimensions, not custom placeholders.

If you need to edit the text itself, this tool is not the right choice. Use a PDF editor or a dedicated PDF-to-PPTX converter that reconstructs text boxes. That kind of conversion is much harder and usually requires a server-side service or a desktop application.

Why the Output Is a Picture, Not Real Text

PDFs store text as positioned glyphs with embedded fonts, not as editable text boxes. To reconstruct that text as editable PowerPoint text runs, you would need to:

  • Detect every paragraph, heading, and list on every page.
  • Map each one to a PowerPoint text frame with the right font, size, colour, and position.
  • Recreate tables, columns, and images as native PowerPoint objects.
  • Deal with fonts that are embedded in the PDF but not installed on the viewer's machine.

That is a hard problem, and any tool that claims to do it perfectly is overstating what is possible. The raster approach used here is honest: it gives you a slide that looks exactly like the PDF page, and it tells you clearly that the text inside is not editable.

Slide Size and Image Quality

Two settings control the output:

  • Slide size — 16:9 widescreen is the default and matches modern projectors and screens. 4:3 standard is useful for older equipment or when the source PDF pages are themselves in a 4:3 ratio.
  • Image quality — Standard (1.5× render scale) produces the smallest file and is fine for screen viewing. High (2×) is the default and looks sharp on most displays. Maximum (3×) produces the largest file but preserves fine detail for print or large-screen projection.

Practical Uses

Turning a report into a presentation: Convert the PDF to PPTX, then add title slides, notes, and highlights on top of each page image. The original layout is preserved exactly.

Reviewing a design proof with a client: Convert the PDF proof to PPTX and annotate each slide with arrows and comments. The client sees the exact artwork.

Archiving a PDF as a slide deck: If you need the content in a presentation format for consistency with other decks, this gives you a one-page-per-slide copy.

Annotating a scanned document: Scanned PDFs work exactly the same way — each scanned page becomes a slide image, and you can mark it up in PowerPoint.

Privacy — Client-Side Conversion

PDF parsing uses the open-source PDF.js library and PPTX generation uses PptxGenJS. Both run entirely in your browser. Your PDF is read into memory and never transmitted. The only external resources loaded are the libraries themselves from a CDN.

PDF to PowerPoint Questions

Can I edit the text in the slides after conversion?

No. Each PDF page is converted to an image, so the text inside the image is not editable. You can add new text boxes, shapes, and annotations on top of each slide in PowerPoint, but you cannot change the original text.

How many slides will my presentation have?

One slide per PDF page. A 20-page PDF becomes a 20-slide presentation. The slides are in the same order as the PDF pages.

What slide sizes are supported?

16:9 widescreen and 4:3 standard. Both are real PowerPoint slide dimensions. Choose the one that matches your projector or your organisation's template.

Will the slides look exactly like the PDF?

Each slide contains a high-resolution image of the corresponding PDF page, so it looks very close to the original. If the PDF page's aspect ratio does not match the slide's aspect ratio, a thin white margin appears on the sides or top and bottom.

Can I change the image quality?

Yes. Choose Standard, High, or Maximum before converting. Higher quality means a larger file and longer conversion time, but sharper images when projected or printed.

Are scanned PDFs supported?

Yes. Scanned PDFs work exactly the same way. Each scanned page becomes a slide image. You can then annotate the slides in PowerPoint.

Does the output open in Google Slides or Keynote?

Yes. The output is a standard .pptx file, which opens in Microsoft PowerPoint, Google Slides, Apple Keynote, and LibreOffice Impress.

Can I convert a password-protected PDF?

No. The converter can only read PDFs that open without a password. If your PDF requires a password, remove the protection first using a dedicated PDF tool, then convert it here.

Why is the file size larger than the original PDF?

Because the output is a set of high-resolution images inside a PPTX container. Images are much larger than the vector text they replace. Choose the Standard quality setting if file size matters.

Is my PDF uploaded to a server?

No. All parsing and conversion happen locally in your browser using PDF.js and PptxGenJS. Your file never leaves your device. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and the tool will continue to work.

Can I add speaker notes to the slides?

Yes, but not in this tool. After downloading the PPTX, open it in PowerPoint and add speaker notes to each slide. The notes are stored separately from the page image and are fully editable.

Can I reorder or delete slides after conversion?

Yes. The downloaded PPTX is a normal presentation file. Open it in PowerPoint and use the slide sorter to reorder, duplicate, or delete slides as needed.
