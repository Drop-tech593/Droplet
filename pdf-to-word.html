What Actually Happens During Conversion

This tool does not take a screenshot of your PDF and paste it into Word. It reads the PDF's text layer, extracts the words with their positions, and rebuilds them as native Word content. The result is a .docx file with real, editable text — not an image.

The process runs in five stages:

  • Read — PDF.js opens the file and reads each page's content stream.
  • Extract — Text items are pulled out with their x/y coordinates, font size, and font name.
  • Group — Items with similar y-coordinates are collected into lines. Lines with similar spacing are joined into paragraphs.
  • Classify — Lines are examined to decide whether they are headings, paragraphs, bullets, numbered items, captions, URLs, or tables.
  • Write — The classified content is written into a DOCX file using the docx library, with each element mapped to an appropriate Word construct.

The whole process runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.

Editable Text — What You Get

Every word extracted from the PDF's text layer becomes editable text in the DOCX. You can click into any paragraph, delete it, retype it, or reformat it — exactly as if you had typed the document in Word yourself.

Text is written using standard Word runs, not as text boxes or shapes. That means it participates in the document's normal flow: it wraps, justifies, and reflows when you change the page size or margins.

What is not preserved:

  • Original fonts. The DOCX uses common fonts — Segoe UI, Calibri, Arial — rather than embedding the fonts from the PDF. If your PDF used a custom typeface, Word will substitute a similar one.
  • Exact positioning. PDFs place characters at precise coordinates. Word uses flowing text. Line breaks and page breaks will differ.
  • Text colour and highlighting. Bold and italic are detected from the PDF's font data, but custom colours and highlight backgrounds are not preserved.
  • Underlines and strikethroughs. These decorations are drawn as lines in the PDF and are not detected as text attributes.

Headings and Lists

Headings are detected by pattern matching and font size. A line becomes a level-1 heading if it looks like 1. Introduction or is in a list of known section names (Executive Summary, References, Appendix). A line becomes a level-2 heading if it looks like 1.2 Subsection.

Headings are rendered as bold paragraphs at 14 pt (level 1) or 12 pt (level 2), coloured dark blue. Word's built-in Heading 1 / Heading 2 styles are not applied, because those styles carry theme colours and spacing that vary between Word versions and would make the output unpredictable.

Bullets are detected when a line starts with a bullet character (•, ●, ▪) and numbered items when a line starts with 1. or 1). Detected lists are rendered with hanging indents so the bullet or number sits to the left of the wrapped text.

Tables

Table detection works by looking for groups of consecutive lines where multiple columns are separated by consistent horizontal gaps. When at least two such lines have their column starts aligned within about 25 pixels, they are treated as a table.

A detected table becomes a real Word table with single-line borders. The first row is treated as a header and rendered bold.

This heuristic works well for simple, evenly spaced tables — for example a financial summary with columns for Item, Qty, and Price. It does not work reliably for:

  • Tables with merged cells spanning multiple columns or rows.
  • Tables with variable cell widths.
  • Tables that span page breaks.
  • Tables with images or complex formatting inside cells.
  • Tables where column starts drift by more than ~25 pixels between rows.

For complex tables, the content is extracted as separate paragraphs — the words are still there and still editable, but the tabular structure is lost.

Images

Embedded raster images — JPEG, PNG, and similar formats — are extracted directly from the PDF's internal object store. Each image is converted to PNG and embedded into the DOCX at its original resolution, capped at 600 × 700 pixels for placement on the page.

Images are placed on their own paragraph, centred. You can move, resize, or replace them in Word, and they retain their quality because they are embedded as full-resolution PNG data.

What is not extracted:

  • Vector graphics. Charts, diagrams, and logos drawn as PDF paths are part of the page content stream, not separate image objects. They cannot be lifted out and will be missing from the DOCX.
  • Background images that cover the entire page as a single object may be skipped if they are very large.
  • Images smaller than 20 × 20 pixels are ignored, as they are usually decorative or noise.
  • Image positioning. Where an image sat relative to surrounding text in the PDF is not preserved — images flow with the text in the DOCX.

Layout — What Changes and Why

PDF is a fixed-layout format. Every character sits at an exact coordinate on a page of a specific size. Word is a reflowable format. Text flows and wraps based on the page size, margins, and available fonts on the reader's machine.

This difference means the output DOCX will not be a pixel-perfect copy of the PDF. Specific changes you should expect:

Element What Happens
Page breaks The converter inserts a page break between PDF pages, but Word's text reflow may push content onto additional lines, shifting later pages.
Margins DOCX uses 0.6 in top/bottom and 0.7 in left/right, tighter than Word's defaults.
Line breaks Lines belonging to the same paragraph are joined. Hard breaks inside a sentence may be lost.
Columns Multi-column PDFs are read left-to-right, top-to-bottom. Columns may occasionally be interleaved.
Headers and footers Removed. DOCX adds its own "Page X of Y" footer.
Page size DOCX uses the default Word page size. Mixed-size PDFs will produce a uniform DOCX.
Hyphenation Words hyphenated across lines in the PDF may remain hyphenated in the DOCX.

If you need a pixel-perfect copy of the PDF, keep the PDF. The DOCX is for editing the content, not for reproducing the layout exactly.

Scanned PDFs — What You Should Know

A scanned PDF is a set of page images with no text layer. There is nothing for the converter to extract, so the DOCX will contain the scanned images but no editable text.

When the converter detects that a page has less than 50 characters of text but contains images, it marks that page as scanned and inserts a warning paragraph at the top of the page in the DOCX. The scanned image itself is still embedded so you can see it.

To make scanned text editable, you need OCR (Optical Character Recognition). That is a separate process from PDF-to-DOCX conversion. Our PDF to TXT converter includes in-browser OCR and can extract text from scanned pages.

Honest Limitations

This tool does a good job on simple, text-heavy documents that were exported from Word, Google Docs, or a publishing system — where the PDF has a real text layer and the layout is mostly single-column paragraphs.

It does not produce a perfect copy of:

  • Magazine layouts with text flowing around images.
  • Complex tables with merged cells.
  • Documents with heavy vector artwork or infographics.
  • Slide decks, forms, or fillable PDFs.
  • Scanned documents (unless OCR is used first).

If your PDF falls into one of these categories, you may need to edit the DOCX manually after conversion, or use a desktop-class PDF-to-Word tool.

Privacy — Client-Side Conversion

PDF parsing uses the open-source PDF.js library and DOCX generation uses the open-source docx library. Both run entirely in your browser. Your PDF is read into memory and never transmitted. The only external resources loaded are the libraries themselves from a CDN.

PDF to DOCX Questions

Will the DOCX look exactly like the PDF?

No. Word reflows text using its own font metrics and line-breaking rules. The layout will be similar — headings, paragraphs, and lists keep their structure — but line breaks and page breaks will shift. For a pixel-perfect copy of the original, keep the PDF.

Is the text really editable in Word?

Yes. The DOCX contains real Word text runs, not images. You can click into any paragraph and change it, format it, delete it, or move it. This is genuine editable content, not a picture of text.

Why doesn't the layout match the PDF exactly?

PDF is a fixed-layout format where every character sits at an exact coordinate. Word is a reflowable format where text flows and wraps based on page size, margins, and available fonts. Reconstructing one from the other is inherently approximate. The converter reproduces the content, not the layout.

Are the fonts in the DOCX the same as in the PDF?

No. The DOCX uses standard fonts (Segoe UI, Calibri, Arial) rather than embedding the fonts from the PDF. If your PDF used a custom font that is not installed on your computer, Word will substitute a similar one. The text stays fully editable.

What happens to tables in the conversion?

Simple, evenly spaced tables are detected and converted to real Word tables with borders and a bold header row. Complex tables — merged cells, nested tables, variable column widths — are not detected. Their content appears as separate paragraphs, so the words are preserved but the tabular structure is lost.

Are images preserved in the DOCX?

Yes, for embedded raster images (JPEG, PNG, and similar). They are extracted at their original resolution and re-embedded as PNG, capped at 600 × 700 pixels for placement. Vector graphics — charts and diagrams drawn as PDF paths — are not extracted.

Why does my scanned PDF produce empty text?

A scanned PDF contains images of pages, not a text layer. There is nothing for the converter to extract. Pages detected as scanned are marked with a warning in the DOCX and the scanned image is embedded, but there is no editable text. To make scanned text editable you need OCR, which is a separate process.

What page margins does the DOCX use?

0.6-inch top and bottom margins and 0.7-inch left and right margins. These are tighter than Word's defaults and closer to typical PDF layouts. You can change them in Word's Layout → Margins menu after opening the file.

Are headers and footers from the PDF kept?

No. Repeating headers and footers (page numbers, running titles) are removed because they are not part of the body content. The DOCX adds its own footer with "Page X of Y" centred at the bottom.

Can I convert a password-protected PDF?

No. The converter can only read PDFs that open without a password. If your PDF requires a password, remove the protection first using a dedicated PDF tool, then convert it here.

How large can my PDF be?

Most modern browsers handle PDFs up to about 100 MB. Very large documents with hundreds of pages or many high-resolution images will take longer and use more memory. If a file fails, try splitting it into smaller parts first.

Is my PDF uploaded to a server?

No. All parsing, analysis, and DOCX generation happen locally in your browser. Your file never leaves your device. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and the tool will continue to work.
