Layer Flattening — What Actually Happens

A PSD file is a stack of layers, each with its own pixels, blending mode, opacity, and visibility flag. To make a PNG, those layers have to be combined into a single image. This process is called compositing or flattening.

The converter reads the PSD's layer structure, applies each layer's blend mode and opacity, respects visibility flags (hidden layers are skipped), and produces the final composite that Photoshop itself would show when the file is open. That composite is then written to a single-layer PNG.

This means the output is not editable as a layered file. You cannot open the PNG in Photoshop and find the original layers inside. If you need to keep the layers editable, keep the PSD. The PNG is for sharing, embedding on the web, or using as a flat asset.

Transparency

PNG supports an alpha channel, so transparent areas of the PSD can be preserved in the output. Two composite modes are offered:

  • Merged on white background — the composite is drawn onto a solid white canvas. Any transparent areas become white. This is the safest choice for images that will be placed on light backgrounds or printed.
  • Merged with transparency — the composite is written with its alpha channel intact. Transparent areas stay transparent in the PNG. Use this for logos, icons, stickers, and any asset that will be layered over other content.

Whether transparency is actually preserved depends on the source PSD. If the PSD was saved with a white background layer at the bottom, the composite is fully opaque and no transparency will appear even in transparent mode. To keep transparency, the PSD must have genuinely transparent regions.

Canvas Dimensions

The output PNG uses the PSD's canvas dimensions by default. If the PSD canvas is 2000 × 1500 pixels, the PNG is 2000 × 1500 pixels. Original size gives the highest fidelity but can produce large files for high-resolution PSDs.

The "Image Size" setting caps the longest edge at a maximum value. Options are 4K (3840 px), 2.5K (2560 px), HD (1920 px), HD Ready (1280 px), and Web (800 px). The shorter edge is scaled proportionally. Aspect ratio is always preserved — no stretching or letterboxing.

For web use, HD (1920 px) is usually more than enough. For social media, 1280 px is often sufficient. For print or archival, use Original Size or 4K.

Colour Modes

Three colour modes are offered:

  • Colour — the composite is written with its original colours. This is the default and preserves the designer's intent.
  • Grayscale — all colours are converted to shades of grey. Useful for documents where colour adds no information, or for saving file size.
  • Sepia — a warm brown tone is applied on top of the greyscale conversion. Useful for vintage-style assets or artistic effects.

Colour mode is applied at render time, after compositing. That means the layer blending happens in full colour first, and only the final image is converted. This is usually the desired behaviour.

PSD Limitations You Should Know

PSD is a complex format, and not every feature can be reproduced by a browser-based parser. Here are the specific limitations of this tool:

  • Layers are flattened, not extracted. You cannot get individual layers as separate PNGs. The output is a single composite image. Extracting each layer to its own PNG is a different operation and is not supported here.
  • Layer effects may not render perfectly. Drop shadows, outer glows, strokes, bevels, and other layer styles are complex and depend on the PSD's internal style data. The parser attempts to render them, but subtle differences from Photoshop are possible.
  • Smart objects and smart filters are not re-evaluated. A smart object contains an embedded source; the parser renders its current appearance, not a re-applied filter chain.
  • Adjustment layers may not fully apply. Some adjustment layers (curves, levels, colour balance) depend on non-destructive editing data that the parser handles with varying accuracy.
  • Text layers render as raster. Text is rendered as pixels, not as live editable text. The output PNG has no font information — the letters are just pixels.
  • Clipping masks are approximated. Simple clipping masks work; complex nested clipping may not render exactly as Photoshop does.
  • 16-bit and 32-bit PSDs may not parse. The tool is tuned for 8-bit documents, which are the most common. High-bit-depth files may fail to open.
  • CMYK and Lab colour modes may be inaccurate. The parser expects RGB. PSDs saved in CMYK or Lab colour may produce incorrect colours.
  • Very large PSDs may be slow or fail. Compositing a multi-gigabyte PSD in the browser is limited by available memory. Files over a few hundred megabytes may not process reliably.
  • Encrypted or password-protected PSDs cannot be read.

Why the Output Is a Flat Image

PNG, like JPEG and WebP, is a single-layer raster format. It has no concept of layers, blend modes, or editable text. The entire purpose of converting PSD to PNG is to produce a flat asset that can be used anywhere — on a website, in an email, in a document, or as a building block in another design. The trade-off is that editability is lost.

If you need both — a layered working file and a flat asset — keep the PSD for editing and use the PNG for distribution. This is the standard workflow in most design teams.

Practical Uses

Exporting a logo for a website: Convert the PSD to a transparent PNG at HD size. The PNG drops directly into the site's header without any background cleanup.

Creating social media assets: Convert the PSD to a Web (800 px) or HD (1920 px) PNG. The flat image is ready to upload to any platform.

Sharing a mockup with a client: Convert the PSD to a PNG and send it as a preview. The client sees the finished design without needing Photoshop.

Archiving finished designs: Keep the PSD as the master file and store a PNG alongside it as a permanent visual record that will always open.

Privacy — Client-Side Conversion

The PSD is read into memory, parsed, composited, and written out as a PNG — all inside your browser. The file never leaves your device. The only external resource loaded is the open-source @webtoon/psd parsing library from a CDN.

PSD to PNG Questions

Is my data safe?

Yes. All parsing and conversion happens locally in your browser. Your PSD files are never uploaded to any server. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and the tool will continue to work.

Are the layers preserved in the PNG?

No. PNG is a single-layer format. The converter composites all visible layers into one flat image and writes that to the PNG. If you need to keep the layers editable, keep the original PSD.

Is transparency preserved?

Yes, if you select "Merged with transparency" and the source PSD has genuinely transparent regions. If the PSD has a white background layer at the bottom, the composite is fully opaque and no transparency will appear.

What are the canvas dimensions of the output?

By default, the PNG uses the PSD's canvas size — a 2000 × 1500 PSD produces a 2000 × 1500 PNG. You can cap the longest edge at 4K, 2.5K, HD, HD Ready, or Web. Aspect ratio is always preserved.

Will layer effects like drop shadows render correctly?

Simple layer effects (drop shadows, glows, strokes) usually render, but subtle differences from Photoshop are possible. Complex effects, nested styles, and effects that depend on non-destructive editing data may not reproduce exactly.

Are text layers still editable?

No. Text layers are rendered as pixels in the PNG. The letters look the same as in Photoshop, but there is no font information and no way to change the text in the output.

Can I extract individual layers as separate PNGs?

Not with this tool. It composites all visible layers into a single image. Extracting each layer to its own PNG is a different operation and is not supported here.

Do 16-bit or 32-bit PSDs work?

The tool is tuned for 8-bit documents, which are the most common. High-bit-depth files may fail to parse or may produce colour inaccuracies. Re-save the PSD as 8-bit in Photoshop if the conversion fails.

What about CMYK PSDs?

The parser expects RGB. PSDs saved in CMYK or Lab colour mode may produce incorrect colours. Convert the PSD to RGB in Photoshop before uploading.

Will I lose image quality?

No. PNG is a lossless format. The composited pixels are written out exactly as they were rendered. There is no re-compression, no JPEG artefacts, no quality loss.

Can I convert multiple PSDs at once?

Yes. Drag and drop multiple PSD files, or use the file picker to select several. Each one is converted independently. You can then download all of them as a single ZIP archive or individually.

Why did my large PSD fail to convert?

Compositing a PSD is memory-intensive. Files above a few hundred megabytes may exceed the browser's available memory. Try reducing the PSD's resolution or flattening complex layer stacks in Photoshop before uploading.
